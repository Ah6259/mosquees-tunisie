Great Mosque of Testour

Testour · around 1630 · Andalusians

Directions See on the map

Overview

Built by Andalusians expelled from Spain in the early 17th century, it blends Tunisian traditions with Hispano-Moorish art. Its minaret bears a clock whose hands turn backwards.

Photos

History

After the expulsion of the Moriscos from Spain (1609-1614), many settled in the Medjerda valley and refounded Testour. The mosque was built in the first third of the 17th century, probably before 1631, by Mohamed Tagharinou, an Andalusian settled in the town. A second courtyard and an ablutions hall were added in the 18th century. In 2014, residents and associations restarted the minaret clock, which had stopped almost three centuries earlier.

Architecture

The plan of the prayer hall (9 aisles) follows the tradition of Kairouan, but the tiled roofs, the stucco decoration and the minaret are Andalusian. 22.50 metres high, the minaret stacks a square tower of brick and stone, in the Toledo manner, and an octagonal tower decorated with ceramics: it recalls the bell towers of Aragon. In the courtyard, a sundial of 1761 showed the prayer times.

Visiting

It is first of all a place of prayer: dress modestly, take off your shoes to enter the prayer hall and avoid prayer times. In several great mosques, non-Muslim visitors may enter the courtyard but not the prayer hall. Opening times change: ask on site.
