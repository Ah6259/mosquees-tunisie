Our presentation

Also called Jamaa El Kebir or the mosque of Abu Miswar, it stands at El Hachène, east of Mellita. According to tradition, it was founded in the 10th century by the Ibadi scholar Abu Miswar and completed by his son Fasil. For centuries it was the island's largest Ibadi school: the rooms of the students and teachers can still be seen. The site covers about 4,000 m², with a square prayer hall and a low minaret; the courtyard keeps several free-standing mihrabs and a sundial from 1790-1791 used to set prayer times. It is part of UNESCO World Heritage (2023).

Overview

La grande mosquée des Banū Yahrāsan, également connue sous le nom de mosquée Boumiswar, Jâma El Hachen ou Jâma Abî Meswer, s'impose comme l'un des monuments religieux ibadites les plus importants de Djerba. Par l'étendue de son enceinte, la présence de nombreuses dépendances et la diversité de ses fonctions, elle dépasse le cadre d'un simple lieu de prière.

The mosques of Djerba

Djerba has several hundred mosques, an exceptional number for an island of 514 km². Most are Ibadi: this branch of Islam settled on the island from the 8th century; Maliki mosques are found mainly in towns such as Houmt Souk. Plain and whitewashed, often topped with domes, with a very low minaret, many also served to defend the island: beach mosques that watched over the coast, fortified mosques inland, underground mosques used as shelters. In 2023 UNESCO inscribed “Djerba: testimony to a settlement pattern in an island territory” on the World Heritage List; it includes 22 mosques.
