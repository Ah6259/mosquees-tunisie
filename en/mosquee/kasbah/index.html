Kasbah Mosque (Tunis)

Tunis · 1231-1235 · Hafsids

Directions See on the map

Overview

Built by the founder of the Hafsid dynasty on Kasbah square, it has one of the most beautiful minarets in Tunis, in the Almohad style of Marrakesh and Seville.

Photos

History

It was built from 1231 to 1235 by order of Abu Zakariya Yahya, founder of the Hafsid dynasty, who had been governor of Seville. At first the court mosque of the rulers living in the Kasbah, it then became one of the city's Friday mosques; it was the first to be built in Tunis after the Zitouna. In the Ottoman period, prayer there followed the Hanafi rite.

Architecture

The prayer hall, deeper than it is wide, has 7 aisles and 9 bays on marble columns with Hafsid capitals; the mihrab is crowned by a muqarnas dome of carved plaster. The square minaret, built as early as 1233, is covered with a network of lozenges in ochre stone: it served as the model for the present minaret of the Zitouna.

Key dates

  2. 1233The minaret is completed

Stories and accounts

Tradition

The white flag of the call to prayer

At prayer time, a white flag is raised at the top of the minaret. It is said to have been a signal to the other mosques: the Kasbah mosque, built around 1230-1235 by the first Hafsid ruler, stands at the highest point of the medina, near the palace, and the city's muezzins began the call when they saw it. The custom has returned in recent years.

Visiting

It is first of all a place of prayer: dress modestly, take off your shoes to enter the prayer hall and avoid prayer times. In several great mosques, non-Muslim visitors may enter the courtyard but not the prayer hall. Opening times change: ask on site.
