Great Mosque of Kairouan (Uqba Mosque)

Kairouan · 670 · present form from 836

Directions See on the map

Overview

The oldest mosque in the Maghreb, founded in 670 together with the city of Kairouan. It served as the model for all the mosques of North Africa and is a UNESCO World Heritage site.

Photos

History

In 670 the conqueror Uqba ibn Nafi founded Kairouan and placed his mosque at the centre of the city. Enlarged and rebuilt in the 8th and 9th centuries, it took its present form in 836 under the Aghlabid emir Ziyadat Allah I. Around 862-863 Abu Ibrahim Ahmad lengthened the prayer hall and gave the minbar and the decoration of the mihrab. In the 11th century the Zirid prince al-Mu'izz ibn Badis added the wooden maqsura. Kairouan was long regarded as the fourth holy city of Islam.

Architecture

A vast quadrilateral of about 9,000 m²: a large paved courtyard, galleries, and a prayer hall of 17 aisles carried by hundreds of ancient columns of marble, granite and porphyry, all different. The minaret, about 31 metres high, dates from the first half of the 9th century: it is one of the oldest minarets still standing. The 9th-century mihrab is decorated with lustre-glazed ceramic tiles; next to it, the carved wooden minbar is the oldest surviving minbar in the Muslim world.

Role and influence

Kairouan was the religious and scholarly capital of Ifriqiya: there, in the 9th century, Imam Sahnun wrote the Mudawwana, the founding work of the Maliki school followed in Tunisia and throughout the Maghreb. The mosque's library preserved very old manuscripts, including leaves of the famous 'Blue Quran'.

Visiting

It is first of all a place of prayer: dress modestly, take off your shoes to enter the prayer hall and avoid prayer times. In several great mosques, non-Muslim visitors may enter the courtyard but not the prayer hall. Opening times change: ask on site.
