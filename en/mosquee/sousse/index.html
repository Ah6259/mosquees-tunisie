Great Mosque of Sousse

Sousse · 850-851 · Aghlabids

Directions See on the map

Overview

At the entrance of the medina stands a fortress-like mosque, built in 850-851 near the ribat that guarded the coast. Sober and powerful, it is part of the Medina of Sousse, a UNESCO World Heritage site.

Photos

History

It was built in 850-851 by the Aghlabid emir Abu al-Abbas Muhammad, some thirty years after the ribat of Sousse; a Kufic inscription on the courtyard galleries records its construction. It was enlarged under Emir Ibrahim II (875-902), when Sousse became the second city of Ifriqiya.

Architecture

Crenellated walls and corner towers topped by small domes: the mosque looks like a fortress and has no real minaret, probably because the watchtower of the nearby ribat played that role. Its prayer hall has 13 aisles; the central aisle and the mihrab bay, both wider, form a 'T' marked by two domes.

Key dates

  1. 821The neighbouring ribat of Sousse is completed
  2. 850Construction of the mosque (850-851)
  3. 875Enlarged under Ibrahim II (875-902)
  4. 1988Medina of Sousse inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage

Visiting

It is first of all a place of prayer: dress modestly, take off your shoes to enter the prayer hall and avoid prayer times. In several great mosques, non-Muslim visitors may enter the courtyard but not the prayer hall. Opening times change: ask on site.
