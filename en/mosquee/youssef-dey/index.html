Youssef Dey Mosque

Tunis · 1612 · Ottomans

Directions See on the map

Overview

The first Hanafi mosque in Tunis, built by the Ottoman dey Youssef near the Kasbah. Its octagonal minaret, the first in Tunis, signals the rite of the Ottomans.

Photos

History

An inscription inside dates its construction to 1612, by order of Youssef Dey, who ruled Tunis from 1610 to 1637. Restored at the end of the 19th century under Ali Bey, it became an annex of the Zitouna university in 1926. The dey rests in a mausoleum there, the first example in Tunis of a funerary mosque.

Architecture

The prayer hall, surrounded by courtyards on three sides, has 9 aisles and 7 bays; a dome rises in front of the mihrab. The minbar is made of masonry faced with coloured marble, whereas Maliki mosques have a wooden minbar. The octagonal minaret ends in a covered balcony and a pyramidal roof of green tiles, like the mausoleum.

Key dates

  1. 1612Built by Youssef Dey (inscription)
  2. 1637Death of Youssef Dey, buried in his mausoleum
  3. 1926Annex of the Zitouna university
  4. 1979The Medina of Tunis inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage

Visiting

It is first of all a place of prayer: dress modestly, take off your shoes to enter the prayer hall and avoid prayer times. In several great mosques, non-Muslim visitors may enter the courtyard but not the prayer hall. Opening times change: ask on site.
