Fadhloun Mosque and the Ibadi mosques of Djerba

Djerba (Midoun) · 14th century · Djerba

Directions See on the map

Overview

On the outskirts of Midoun stands a white mosque that looks like a small fortress, typical of the island of Djerba, home to an Ibadi Muslim community. It has been a listed monument since 2022 and is among the Djerba sites inscribed as World Heritage in 2023.

History

Built in the 14th century, the Fadhloun mosque belongs to the rich group of Djerba mosques, often Ibadi, built among the fields and along the coast. With their thick walls and small minarets, many also served as lookout posts: they formed a second line of defence behind the coastal forts.

Architecture

The square prayer hall, about 7.5 metres per side, is covered by nine small domes carried by four columns; the mihrab is a simple niche and the stone minbar has only three steps. A squat two-level minaret rises above the roof. Around it: a courtyard with a 'summer mihrab', a rainwater cistern, two rooms for Quran teaching, an underground bakery and a grain mill.

Role and influence

These mosques were small centres of life: prayer, Quran teaching, bread and grain for the neighbourhood. Their bare white architecture is one of the faces of Djerba recognised by UNESCO in 2023.

Stories and accounts

Historical factA mosque that fed its neighbourhood

The Fadhloun mosque on Djerba probably dates from the 14th century. Originally Ibadi, it was part of a line of fortified mosques along the coast. It had its own Quranic school, grain mill and bakery, whose income paid its costs, and it supplied water from its cistern to the neighbouring houses.

Source: Patrimoine de Tunisie

TraditionAs many mosques as days in the year

A Djerba saying claims the island has as many mosques as there are days in the year. The old Ibadi mosques, like Fadhloun, are small and plain, built with local materials by and for the people of the hamlets, sometimes for only a dozen worshippers. They must be whitewashed with lime regularly, or the stone decays. As a rule they had no minaret, except to watch the coast.

Source: Les clés du Moyen-Orient (V. Prevost)

Historical factMosques as the island's ramparts

On Djerba, mosques also protected the people. Those on the coast formed the first line: from them the sea was watched and the alarm given. Fortified mosques, like Fadhloun, formed a second line of defence, while those inland, some dug underground, served as refuges and also hosted teaching. In 2023 UNESCO inscribed Djerba on the World Heritage List for this island settlement pattern, of which these mosques are a part.

Source: Patrimoine de Tunisie · UNESCO

Visiting

It is first of all a place of prayer: dress modestly, take off your shoes to enter the prayer hall and avoid prayer times. In several great mosques, non-Muslim visitors may enter the courtyard but not the prayer hall. Opening times change: ask on site.
