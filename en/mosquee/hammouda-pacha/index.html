Hammouda Pacha Mosque

Tunis · 1655 · Muradids

Directions See on the map

Overview

The second Hanafi mosque in Tunis, built in 1655 by Hammouda Pacha Bey. Its slender octagonal minaret overlooks Sidi Ben Arous street in the medina.

Photos

History

Hammouda Pacha, bey of the Muradid dynasty, had the mosque built in 1655 at the corner of Sidi Ben Arous street and Kasbah street. Like Youssef Dey before him, he had his own mausoleum built in its courtyard: this tomb is said to have inspired, much later, that of President Bourguiba in Monastir.

Architecture

A 17th-century Ottoman building: a marble door, green-tiled roofs and three courtyards. The prayer hall, about 24 × 17 metres, has 7 aisles on 48 columns; its walls are faced with marble and carved plaster, with floral decoration and coloured marble of Italian influence. Unusually for Tunis, the courtyard opens onto the street through wide grilled windows. The octagonal minaret is topped by a balcony and a pyramidal roof.

Visiting

It is first of all a place of prayer: dress modestly, take off your shoes to enter the prayer hall and avoid prayer times. In several great mosques, non-Muslim visitors may enter the courtyard but not the prayer hall. Opening times change: ask on site.
