Great Mosque of Monastir

Monastir · 9th century · enlarged until the 18th

Directions See on the map

Overview

Facing the sea, next to the famous ribat, stands a sober and elegant stone mosque, enlarged in every period since the 9th century.

Photos

History

The present prayer hall is the result of four stages: a small vaulted oratory in the 9th century, an enlargement in the 11th century (Zirid period) with the mihrab, then in the Hafsid period a square minaret and two more aisles. An outer gallery was added in the 18th century. In October 2013, two earth tremors caused the top of the minaret to partly collapse.

Architecture

Façades of dressed stone, enlivened by the frames of the doors and windows and by the arches of a gallery. The mosque has no courtyard in front of the prayer hall. The 11th-century mihrab, with Zirid decoration and floriated Kufic script, is not topped by a dome, which is rare in medieval Ifriqiya.

Stories and accounts

Historical factScholars on retreat by the sea

The Great Mosque of Monastir, with its vaulted 9th-century hall, stands next to the ribat, a fortress where the coast was guarded and where people prayed. The scholars of Kairouan came there on retreat, between prayer, study and watching over the sea.

Source: Chapoutot-Remadi, Afkar/Idées (IEMed)

Historical factImam al-Mazari rests in Monastir

Imam al-Mazari (1061-1141), one of the greatest Maliki scholars, lived in Mahdia, where he died. Yet he was buried in Monastir, the town of the ribat and the Great Mosque, where his mausoleum is still visited. A student of al-Lakhmi of Sfax, he wrote al-Mu'lim, a commentary on Imam Muslim's collection of hadiths. Malikis simply call him “the Imam”.

Source: Wikipedia

Visiting

It is first of all a place of prayer: dress modestly, take off your shoes to enter the prayer hall and avoid prayer times. In several great mosques, non-Muslim visitors may enter the courtyard but not the prayer hall. Opening times change: ask on site.
