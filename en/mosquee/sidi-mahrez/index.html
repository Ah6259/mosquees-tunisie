Sidi Mahrez Mosque (Mohamed Bey Mosque)

Tunis · 1692-1697 · Muradids

Directions See on the map

Overview

With its white domes, which appear on the emblem of the city of Tunis, it is the only Tunisian mosque built on the model of the great mosques of Istanbul. It faces the zawiya of Sidi Mahrez, patron saint of the medina.

Photos

History

Mohamed Bey el-Mouradi began building it between 1692 and 1697, in honour of Sidi Mahrez (Mahrez ibn Khalaf, 10th century), in the Bab Souika quarter. The political troubles of the late 17th century prevented the work from being completed: the mosque never received a minaret. It has been restored since 1984, together with the Bab Souika quarter.

Architecture

Set on a platform, the square prayer hall (28 metres per side) is covered by a large central dome nearly 16 metres across, surrounded by half-domes and small corner domes, like the Sultan Ahmed Mosque in Istanbul. Iznik ceramic tiles in blue, green and 'tomato red' decorate the pillars and the mihrab wall.

Key dates

  1. 1692Construction begun by Mohamed Bey el-Mouradi
  2. 1697Work ends, without a minaret
  3. 1984Restoration with the Bab Souika quarter

Visiting

It is first of all a place of prayer: dress modestly, take off your shoes to enter the prayer hall and avoid prayer times. In several great mosques, non-Muslim visitors may enter the courtyard but not the prayer hall. Opening times change: ask on site.
