Great Mosque of Sfax

Sfax · around 850 · Aghlabids

Directions See on the map

Overview

In the heart of the medina of Sfax stands a 9th-century mosque whose eastern façade, carved in the Zirid period, and whose minaret, modelled on Kairouan's, are masterpieces of the art of Ifriqiya.

Photos

History

The first building dates from around 850, under the Aghlabids, at the same time as the ramparts of Sfax. The mosque was embellished in the Zirid period: two inscriptions on the façade bear the dates 988 and 1085. Altered in the 12th century, it regained its original dimensions in the 18th century during major works.

Architecture

The eastern façade is punctuated by doors and windows with horseshoe arches, niches and a dentilled cornice; a Byzantine plaque carved with two peacocks is reused there. The square minaret, about fifteen metres high, recalls Kairouan's but has finer decoration: carved discs and beautiful floriated Kufic script. The L-shaped prayer hall has 9 aisles.

Key dates

  1. 850First building, under the Aghlabids (around this date)
  2. 988Zirid embellishment (façade inscription)
  3. 1085Second dated inscription on the façade
  4. 1912Listed as a historic monument

Visiting

It is first of all a place of prayer: dress modestly, take off your shoes to enter the prayer hall and avoid prayer times. In several great mosques, non-Muslim visitors may enter the courtyard but not the prayer hall. Opening times change: ask on site.
