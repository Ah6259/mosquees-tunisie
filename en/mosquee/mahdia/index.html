Great Mosque of Mahdia

Mahdia · 916 · Fatimids

Directions See on the map

Overview

Built in 916 by the first Fatimid caliph in his new capital, on the seashore. Its great projecting portal, inspired by triumphal arches, was a first in mosque architecture.

Photos

History

When Ubayd Allah al-Mahdi founded Mahdia in 909, he had the mosque built near the caliphal palace, on a platform reclaimed from the sea. After the Spanish destroyed the city in 1554, the building was often altered. Between 1961 and 1965 it was almost entirely rebuilt according to the 10th-century plan; the great portal and the northern portico are original.

Architecture

A quadrilateral of about 85 × 55 metres with massive walls, flanked by two square towers that served as rainwater tanks. The mosque never had a minaret. The monumental portal opened a 'path of honour' towards the mihrab, at the far end of a 9-aisle hall whose higher central aisle leads to a dome.

Visiting

It is first of all a place of prayer: dress modestly, take off your shoes to enter the prayer hall and avoid prayer times. In several great mosques, non-Muslim visitors may enter the courtyard but not the prayer hall. Opening times change: ask on site.
