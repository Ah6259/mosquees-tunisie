How does the site work?

A free, independent website to find a mosque anywhere in Tunisia and discover the history of the country's great mosques.

What is the site for?

Find a mosque or a prayer room, in Tunisia and anywhere in the world, and discover the history of the great mosques. The site is free, with no account and no advertising.

How to use it: each feature in one line

  • Map: every place of prayer in Tunisia. Tap a point to see its name, directions (Google Maps) and its page.
  • A mosque's page: photo, names in several languages, Wikipedia summary, small map and directions.
  • Search: type the name of a mosque or a town; similar spellings are understood (Jerba = Djerba).
  • Filters: mosques, prayer rooms, or only the places that have a history page.
  • “Near me”: the places closest to you, with their distance.
  • The whole world: search for a town (Mecca, Istanbul, Paris…) or tap “See the world”; each country has its own colour.
  • Report a place: add a missing mosque or prayer room by placing its marker on the map.
  • “It's true” or “No longer exists”: confirm a reported place, or say that it has gone.
  • Great mosques: history, architecture, photos, dates and sources of the great mosques of Tunisia and the other historic mosques.
  • Great mosques of the world: a search (Tunisia and the world) and a “Sort” button (oldest, newest, alphabetical order…).
  • Three pages to read: Role of the mosque, Stories of the mosques, Build a mosque.
  • Quran verses: in Arabic, with a recognised translation of their meaning (QuranEnc), and a link to the Quran on our site The Message.
  • Recitation by Sheikh Ali El Barrak: the sound only starts after your choice; the round button in the header stops it.
  • 21 languages: the language button in the header opens the same page in your language.
  • Share: the button in the header opens the phone's share menu (otherwise WhatsApp).
  • On your phone: add the site to the home screen like an app; pages you have already opened stay readable offline.
  • Presentation video: a short video that shows the site.
  • Your feedback: a remark or a mistake? Write to us, every message is read.

Where do the places come from?

  • OpenStreetMap, the free map made by its contributors: every Muslim place of prayer in Tunisia, with its position and name, re-read every 3 months by our robot.
  • Wikidata: for mosques that have a record there, their names in several languages, founding date, protection as a monument, photo and Wikipedia articles.
  • Visitors: anyone can report a missing mosque or prayer room. It is published automatically (about every 2 hours), marked “to be confirmed” until another visitor confirms it.
  • Elsewhere in the world: OpenStreetMap is read live for the area shown on the map (5 copies of OpenStreetMap, the Overpass servers), each country with its own colour. A town anywhere in the world is found with Nominatim (© OpenStreetMap contributors), only when you confirm the search.

The texts

The pages of the great mosques are written by us, from reliable sources cited at the bottom of each page (Wikipedia, Qantara, Discover Islamic Art, UNESCO). The other pages show the summary of the Wikipedia article, cited with its link and licence (CC BY-SA 4.0). The Quran verse is given in Arabic with a recognised translation of its meaning (QuranEnc).

How a reported place is checked

  • The robot rejects a position outside Tunisia or an unknown type, removes phone numbers and links (no advertising), and limits the number of reports per phone.
  • A place reported less than 40 m from a known place counts as a confirmation (no duplicates).
  • If several visitors say “no longer exists” (more than the confirmations), the place is removed from the map.

The recitation

The voice is that of Sheikh Ali El Barrak (1899-1981), a great Tunisian reciter of the Quran. The videos come from his official YouTube channel and are only embedded (played by YouTube, through the youtube-nocookie.com domain): nothing is copied. The sound only starts after your choice; the round button in the header stops or restarts it. « الشيخ علي البراق / Cheikh Ali Al-Barrak »

Your data

Your position is only used to show nearby places, inside your phone: it is sent only if you report a place (and then only the position of that place). An anonymous browser identifier prevents the same person from voting several times. Anonymous visit statistics, no cookies. No advertising.

Outside Tunisia, the area shown on the map (not your exact position) is sent to the OpenStreetMap servers to read its mosques; a town searched for in the world is sent to Nominatim.

Automatic updates

Everything happens on its own, through our robots: places are re-read every 3 months, reported places are published about every 2 hours, and every change to the site is tested before it is published.

An independent site

This site is not an official service and does not depend on any mosque or organisation. Prayer times and opening to visitors change: ask on site.
